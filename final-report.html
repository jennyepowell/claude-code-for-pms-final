The short version

4.2 changed two things at once, and four responders stopped hearing from us.

On 12 Aug, release 4.2 shortened the ping wait from 90s to 60s and weighted proximity up against acceptance history. Callout tickets jumped from ~6.5 to ~26 a week and stayed there.

Pings that nobody answered in time (missed) cost a responder's ranking the same as a "no" (turned down). A lower rank means fewer pings. The score only moves when you are pinged, so a responder who drops can't climb back.

What isn't proven: the 60s wait clearly caused misses. Whether it, the weight change, or something else collapsed the four responders' rank is not confirmed.

Module 5 · Super Speed

The fix: stop punishing misses, show why, and give a way back.

My prompt
Let's have misses count for zero - so we can say "misses don't negatively impact your ranking status; only turned down pings bring your ranking status down" or something along those lines. Phrase it as you see fit
05-super-speed/prompts.md · prompt 29

05-super-speed/brief.md: three changes, together

  • Kip sees why. A ranking status (Low / Fair / Good) plus the last five pings and how each ended. No raw score.
  • Meteor Mite gets a way back. A callout they'd rank for, with a note saying why.
  • A miss counts for zero, past ones included. One-time recalculation at release, for every responder.

Not in scope: reverting 4.2, changing the 60s wait or the weights, handler overrides. Success is proposed, not agreed: each of the four back to ~12+ pings a week, quiet-phone tickets ≤ ~5 in four weeks.

Module 6 · Sidekicks

The skill that checks the next brief before it ships.

My prompt (this session)
Turn that into a skill called review-checklist, so I can point it at any brief and get the same check every time, without me explaining it again.
Not in 06-sidekicks/prompts.md: its three prompt slots are empty. This one was typed in the session that built the skill.

The checklist is six questions: customer experience today, the problem, an owner, how we'll know, scope start vs end, and problem before fix. It is saved at .claude/skills/review-checklist/SKILL.md.

Why it matters for 4.2: nobody wrote down how pings are decided, the roadmap went stale, and there is no baseline to say what "worked" looks like. A brief that has to state its problem with evidence, a measure and an owner before it goes forward is harder to ship on opinion.

What it does not do: it reviews briefs. It won't catch a bug in the code, and it can't prove 4.2's own brief lacked these (I haven't seen it).
